Memory kept for the backward pass

What to try. Plain: memory fills with every layer's input. Coupling and midpoint with exact arithmetic: every input comes back exactly, from the final state alone. Switch to the 3-digit calculator: the rebuilt inputs drift, worst at the bottom. Switch to fixed point (whole hundredths): exact again. (Its grid is the same size for every number, so the start, 0.0137, is stored as 0.01. Our model's grid is 2-24, about 0.00000006, far finer than its smallest stream numbers.) Euler: the guesses close in for layers 4 and 2, and fly apart in the steep layer 3, after which everything below it is wrong. Momentum: the divide by 0.9 makes the 3-digit errors grow.

The real model does the same with 384 numbers per token, 256 tokens per sequence and 10 layers. Its fixed-point unit is 2-24, not a hundredth.
